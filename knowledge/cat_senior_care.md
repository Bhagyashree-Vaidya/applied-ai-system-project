## When is a cat senior
Cats are generally considered senior from around 11 years old. Veterinarians commonly recommend wellness checkups every 6 months for senior cats because problems progress faster.

## Thirst and urination changes
Drinking noticeably more water or urinating more than usual can be a sign of kidney disease, diabetes or hyperthyroidism, which are common in older cats. This always warrants a veterinary visit and usually blood and urine tests.

## Hydration for kidney health
Wet food, water fountains and several water bowls around the home help cats drink more, which supports kidney health. Cats with kidney disease often eat a vet-prescribed renal diet.

## Medication routines
Give medications at the same times every day as prescribed. Pill pockets, or hiding a pill in a small amount of wet food, can make dosing easier. Never change a dose or stop a medication without talking to your vet.

## Arthritis and mobility
Senior cats often have arthritis even when they do not limp. Low-sided litter boxes, ramps or steps to favorite spots, and soft warm bedding help. Ask your vet about pain relief; many human pain medications are toxic to cats.

## Grooming help
Older cats groom less thoroughly. Short, gentle brushing sessions of 5-10 minutes help prevent mats and let you check for lumps.
