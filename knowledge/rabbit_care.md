## Hay and diet
Unlimited fresh grass hay (such as timothy) should make up roughly 80-85% of a rabbit's diet. Add a daily portion of leafy greens and only a small measured amount of pellets. Fresh water must always be available.

## GI stasis is an emergency
A rabbit that stops eating or stops producing droppings for around 12 hours may have GI stasis, which can be fatal. Contact a vet experienced with rabbits right away; do not wait to see if it passes.

## Activity patterns
Rabbits are crepuscular: they are most active at dawn and dusk and often rest during the middle of the day. Plan play and exercise time for early morning or evening.

## Exercise space
Rabbits need several hours a day outside their enclosure in a safe, rabbit-proofed area to run, jump and explore.

## Enrichment ideas
Bored rabbits benefit from cardboard boxes and tunnels to hide in, a dig box filled with shredded paper or soil, safe chew toys such as untreated willow, and foraging by scattering greens or hiding hay in paper bags or toilet-roll tubes.

## Grooming
Brush rabbits more often during shedding seasons, since they cannot vomit swallowed fur. Rabbits should not be bathed; spot-clean instead.
